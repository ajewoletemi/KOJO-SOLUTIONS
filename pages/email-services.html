How to Get & Setup Inbox-Receivable Business Emails

Step-by-step guide

Step 1: Get a Domain Name

Buy a domain (e.g. yourbusiness.com) from Namecheap, Google Domains, Cloudflare, or similar. Choose a name that matches your brand.

Step 2: Choose an Email Provider

Popular reliable options for business email:

  • Google Workspace — Best overall, Gmail interface, excellent deliverability
  • Microsoft 365 — Great for Outlook users and Office integration
  • Zoho Mail — Affordable and solid free tier available
  • Proton Mail / Tutanota — Privacy-focused options
  • Cloudflare Email Routing + forwarding — Free forwarding to your personal inbox

Step 3: Set Up DNS Records (Critical for Inbox Delivery)

After signing up with a provider, they will give you DNS records to add at your domain registrar:

  • MX records — Tell the internet where to deliver email for your domain
  • SPF record — Authorizes which servers can send email as you
  • DKIM — Cryptographic signature that proves the email is legitimate
  • DMARC — Policy that tells receivers what to do with unauthenticated mail

Correct SPF + DKIM + DMARC dramatically improves inbox placement and reduces spam folder risk.

Step 4: Create Mailboxes & Test

Create addresses like:

  • info@yourdomain.com
  • support@yourdomain.com
  • yourname@yourdomain.com

Send test emails to Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo. Check that they land in the inbox. Use tools like mail-tester.com to score your setup.

Step 5: Best Practices for Receivable Emails

  • Never send bulk email from your main business address without proper warm-up
  • Keep a clean contact list and honor unsubscribes
  • Use a consistent "From" name and reply-to address
  • Avoid spam trigger words in subject lines when possible
  • Monitor your domain reputation with Google Postmaster Tools
